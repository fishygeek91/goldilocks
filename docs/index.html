Noise cannot help quantum-enhanced MCMC

Exactness is free, advantage is not.

Dustin Kovac · Independent researcher, Pinedale, Wyoming · 2026

Read the paper (PDF) Archive · DOI Code · goldilocks Code · tunnelvision

A famous trick in photosynthesis says a little noise can help energy move through a molecule. Does the same trick help a quantum computer propose better moves inside Markov-chain Monte Carlo — the workhorse sampling algorithm of physics, optimization, and machine learning? On today's noisy hardware the hope is seductive: the sampler stays exact under noise, and a little dephasing can make a quantum walk mix faster. This paper settles it, in two provable halves.

The result, in two halves

Exactness is free. Every transpose-closed noise channel — dephasing in any real basis, depolarizing noise, infinite-temperature relaxation — preserves the sampler's evaluation-free exactness at any strength. Amplitude damping (T₁) is the one realistic exception, with a bounded bias. The noise that dominates today's devices is exactly the free kind.

Advantage is not. Intermediate dephasing really does create "Goldilocks" peaks in the walk's own mixing — but a pre-registered test on fifty random instances finds those peaks never lift the sampler above the envelope of the coherent quench and simple classical kernels (49/50 cells, one disclosed +1.17% exception). Where the quantum proposal already wins, noise erodes it; where noise helps the walk, a classical method was already faster. Noise costs nothing and buys nothing.

Also in v1.0.0

A free-certificate theorem: a conserved diagonal charge confines every measured outcome to its symmetry sector at zero cost while preserving evaluation-free exactness within it — the classical version of that guarantee is a completeness certificate that scales badly. And a pre-registered PORTAL race that kills the natural amortized device-mined-library architecture on classical grounds, reported at full strength as a registered negative.
